# Hardware

The KiCad project for PCB v1 (milestone M1b, after the [breadboard prototype](../docs/breadboard.md)): schematic, layout and the JLCPCB order files, all generated by scripts from one netlist. Not built yet. The design has been checked against the data sheets once ([what the review changed](#review-before-the-first-order)); [three things](#check-before-ordering) are left to check on the bench and on JLCPCB's order page.

## PCB v1

| File | What |
|---|---|
| [`tools/design.py`](tools/design.py) | **the circuit** – every part with its value, LCSC number and the net on each pin. Edit this, not the schematic |
| [`tools/gen_schematic.py`](tools/gen_schematic.py) | writes `doormutex.kicad_sch` from it, runs KiCad's ERC and checks that the exported netlist is exactly the design |
| [`lib/`](lib/) | the JLCPCB parts as a project library: symbols and footprints imported from their LCSC numbers with [easyeda2kicad](https://github.com/uPesy/easyeda2kicad.py), so footprint and placement match what JLCPCB assembles. The 3D models (~40 MB) are not in the repository – the same command with `--full` fetches them into `lib/doormutex.3dshapes/`. [`tools/fix_lib.py`](tools/fix_lib.py) corrects the pin types EasyEDA gets wrong (passive parts marked as inputs) |
| `doormutex.kicad_sch` | generated: one A1 sheet, a block per function, net labels on every pin |
| [`tools/gen_pcb.py`](tools/gen_pcb.py) | the board: outline, 4 layers, every part placed by hand-picked coordinates (`PLACE`), the tracks that carry current laid by hand (`ROUTES`), checks that the antenna, the USB-C opening and the INFO button face outwards |
| [`tools/route_pcb.py`](tools/route_pcb.py) | routes the rest with [Freerouting](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting) (needs Java 25+) around the hand-laid tracks, ground pours, DRC |
| [`tools/check_power.py`](tools/check_power.py) | the copper resistance of every power path, from the tracks and vias; fails above a limit per path (run by `route_pcb.py`) |
| [`tools/export_scad.py`](tools/export_scad.py) | writes every part of the board as a box into [`enclosure/doormutex.scad`](../enclosure/doormutex.scad) for its collision check, and compares the enclosure's board parameters with `gen_pcb.py` |
| [`tools/export_jlc.py`](tools/export_jlc.py) | `production/`: Gerber zip, BOM and CPL for JLCPCB (pad centres and bottom-side rotations the way JLCPCB counts them) |
| `doormutex.kicad_pro`, `doormutex.kicad_dru` | design rules (JLCPCB 4-layer, 0.127 mm), net classes (Power 0.5 mm, USB) |

```bash
python3 hardware/tools/gen_schematic.py      # needs kicad-cli (KiCad 9 or 10)
python3 hardware/tools/gen_pcb.py            # replaces the board – placement lives in PLACE
python3 hardware/tools/route_pcb.py          # Freerouting + DRC (0 errors) + power paths
python3 hardware/tools/export_scad.py        # the board's parts for the enclosure's collision check
python3 hardware/tools/export_jlc.py         # production/
# a new JLCPCB part (from hardware/, so the 3D model's path is relative to the project):
(cd hardware && easyeda2kicad --full --project-relative --output "$PWD/lib/doormutex" --lcsc_id C2845237)
python3 hardware/tools/fix_lib.py
```

### Layout

41 × 191.5 mm, 4 layers (inner layer 2, right below the parts on the back, a solid ground plane; inner layer 1 for routing), the board is the back wall of the three chambers in the enclosure's PCB build (`build = "pcb"` in [`enclosure/doormutex.scad`](../enclosure/doormutex.scad)).

| Side | What |
|---|---|
| Front (towards the lenses) | the LED pairs in the middle of each reflector cone, the light sensor in the top corner of the green chamber (a pinhole and a hood in the front shell), the e-paper socket in the sign, and a 2 mm wide 3.3 V track from the buck-boost up to the ESP32 |
| Back | ESP32-C6 at the top with its antenna past the top edge (plastic-only roof), the 18650 holder in the middle, lamp drivers left of it, cell protection, sense resistor and fuel gauge right of its minus end, the cell's plus lead (0 Ω, PTC, reverse-polarity MOSFET) below it on the left on its way to the charger, buck-boost and supervisor at the bottom right, USB-C and the side-actuated INFO button on the bottom edge |

- **Current paths** are laid by hand in `gen_pcb.py` on the outer layers (35 µm copper; JLCPCB's inner layers have half of that) and checked by `check_power.py`: USB-C → charger 24 mΩ, cell → charger 19 mΩ in the plus lead and 10 mΩ in the minus lead, charger → buck-boost 13 mΩ, 3.3 V → ESP32 43 mΩ (22 mV at a 0.5 A Wi-Fi peak). The fuel gauge measures across the sense resistor's pads with its own two tracks. What else hangs on these nets – test points, sense lines, the lamps' supply, 3.3 V for sensor and e-paper – is the autorouter's: 0.5 mm where that fits, 0.25 mm where it does not, never less.
- **Buck-boost** as in the data sheet's layout: the coil at the two switch pins, input and output capacitor at the two ends of the pin row, ground vias next to them.
- **E-paper:** the Waveshare module hangs upside down (its PH2.0 socket at the top; `menuconfig → E-ink → connector at the top`), the right-angle PH socket J2 sits above it, opening down. Needed: a **PH2.0 8-pin cable with a socket on both ends, 1:1** (~10 cm, single wires, not a flat cable). J2 is through-hole and soldered by hand, as is nothing else. The cable leaves the module upwards, turns over in the 9 mm below the chamber wall and goes down behind the module, where its slack lies; from there it comes up into J2. J2's pins are in the module's order – each pin of the module's socket is right below the same signal on J2, `BUSY` and `3V3` are printed at J2's two ends.
- **USB-C:** the socket's mouth is 0.6 mm past the board's bottom edge (the data sheet draws the edge another 0.4 mm back), 0.1 mm above the enclosure's bottom wall.
- **Mounting:** four M2.5 self-tapping screws into bosses of the front shell – nylon for the top two, they are 11 mm from the antenna.
- **Assembly:** both sides at JLCPCB (the LEDs and the light sensor are on the front) – that is their *Standard* PCBA. The ESP32's antenna reaches 4.9 mm past the top edge and parts sit close to the long edges: let JLCPCB add edge rails and fiducials.

### Before the firmware runs on it

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| e-paper RST → GPIO 14, BUSY → GPIO 15 | the MINI module has no GPIO 10/11 |
| `connector at the top` for the e-ink | the module hangs upside down |
| charger CHG → GPIO 7, PGOOD → GPIO 17 (both are off by default) | as on the breadboard's stage 2; open drain, the firmware's internal pull-ups are the only ones |
| fuel gauge driver for the MAX17055 (ModelGauge m5 EZ: DesignCap, IChgTerm 100 mA, VEmpty, ModelCfg after every power-on reset) with a **20 mΩ** sense resistor: 78.125 µA and 0.25 mAh per bit, half of the data sheet's 10 mΩ figures | replaces the MAX17048; same address 0x36. The gauge is reset whenever the protection IC has cut the cell off |
| LED supply: SYS is **4.4 V while USB is plugged in** (PGOOD low), otherwise the cell voltage; LED reference voltage ≈ 3600 mV; forward voltages and levels per colour from the XINGLIGHT LEDs | LEDs run from SYS (3.0–4.4 V). Today the firmware takes the cell voltage for the supply – on USB the lamps would be too bright |
| after a start, read the cell voltage before Wi-Fi comes up; below about 3.3 V on the cell alone go back to sleep | near the 3.0 V cut-off the Wi-Fi start pulls SYS below it again: a restart loop that drains what is left |
| light sensor: a factor for the pinhole (calibrate once against the breadboard's sensor) | the sensor sees the room through a 2.4 mm hole 16 mm away, a fraction of the light an open sensor gets |
| INFO button on GPIO 5 (to ground, 10 kΩ pull-up), gauge alert on GPIO 4; both can wake the chip from deep sleep | new |

Nothing to change: LEDs on GPIO 2/3/6, I²C on 22/23, e-paper DIN/CLK/CS/DC on 18–21, USB on 12/13 are the breadboard's pins. GPIO 8 and 9 have their pull-ups, 9 has the BOOT button. Three of the pins the breadboard kept free as strapping pins are in use now – 4 and 5 (they only set the SDIO clock edge) and 15 for BUSY (it only selects the JTAG source once the JTAG eFuses are burnt). The 32 kHz crystal is not fitted. The 8 MB module runs the 4 MB partition table as it is.

### Review before the first order

A second pass over the design, on 2026-11-27, with the data sheets open. What it found and changed:

| Found | Changed |
|---|---|
| **Placement file:** JLCPCB counts the rotation of a bottom-side part looking at the bottom (180° minus KiCad's angle) and places by the centre of the pads. The export wrote KiCad's angle and the footprint origin – 26 parts on the back, every IC among them, would have been placed turned by 180° | `export_jlc.py`; verified from the pad positions alone, without KiCad's angles |
| **3.3 V to the ESP32:** 0.37 Ω – 171 mm of a 0.5 mm track on the thin inner layer, 180 mV at a Wi-Fi peak. That is the breadboard's reset loop again, in copper. USB-C → charger and cell → charger were 0.12 Ω each | hand-laid tracks on the outer layers, `check_power.py` (figures above); the cell's plus lead moved to the left, next to the charger's BAT pins |
| **Buck-boost:** the autorouter had taken one coil connection through two vias and 7 mm of 0.15 mm track, and both capacitors through vias and the inner layer | placed and connected as in the data sheet |
| **Ground plane** on inner layer 1, 1.3 mm away from the parts on the back | inner layer 2, 0.2 mm below them |
| **Charger:** no vias in the thermal pad (1–2 W while charging) | four |
| **Light sensor:** VEML7700-**TR** is the side-view reel, the sensor would have looked along the board; and the light channel's tube ended 1.2 mm above the board where a 2.35 mm tall part sits | VEML7700-TT (C1850416), a hood around it in the front shell |
| **USB-C** 1.25 mm inside the board edge instead of past it: behind the 1.8 mm bottom wall a plug would not have gone in all the way | mouth 0.6 mm past the edge, recess in the wall to match |
| **E-paper socket** J2 had the signals in the order of the module's print, 3.3 V at pin 1. The module's socket has `VCC` at JST's pin 8: a 1:1 cable would have put 3.3 V on BUSY, ground on RST and so on | J2's pins in the module's order, pin 1 = BUSY … pin 8 = 3.3 V |
| J2 also reached 0.45 mm into the wall between sign and green chamber (the enclosure's dummy for it was smaller than the part) | 1.1 mm lower; the enclosure's collision check now uses every part of the real board |
| **EN pull-up** 10 MΩ: with the data sheets' worst-case leakage (0.2 µA into EN, 0.09 µA into the supervisor's output) EN stays below its threshold | 1 MΩ |
| **Green LED** with 68 Ω: up to 28 mA at full duty on USB, rated 20 | 100 Ω |
| **Charge timer:** with TMR open the charger gives up after 4–6 h. Behind the PTC, two MOSFETs and the sense resistor a worn or 3400 mAh cell spends long in the constant-voltage phase and can need 4–4.5 h | 47 kΩ at TMR: 4.7–7.8 h |
| Input capacitor of the buck-boost 10 µF (4–5 µF left at 4.4 V, the data sheet's minimum), BAT capacitor 4.7 µF | 22 µF, 10 µF |
| No copper allowed under the INFO switch's plunger (data sheet); ground reached under the ESP32's antenna end | keep-out areas |
| The holder's polarity marks are under the holder | `+` and `−` beside it, labels at J2 and the buttons |

Checked and left as it was: every pin of BQ24074, DW01A, FS8205A (both MOSFETs the right way round), DMP2035U, MAX17055, TPS3840, TPS63802, USBLC6, AO3400A, VEML7700 and the ESP32-C6-MINI-1 against its data sheet; the feedback divider (511 k / 91 k is the data sheet's own pair for 3.3 V) and the coil (in its list); the strapping pins; the three LEDs' polarity (red and yellow number their pads the other way round than green – the footprints agree with the data sheets); buttons and cell holder against their drawings; the I²C addresses.

### Check before ordering

- **The e-paper cable.** J2's pin order was taken from photos of the module (its socket has `BUSY` at JST's pin 1, `VCC` at pin 8), not from a module on the bench. With the cable in the module and nothing powered, measure it: the wire from the module's `VCC` pad must end at the pin marked `3V3` on the board, the one from `BUSY` at `BUSY` – in the light they run straight up, without crossing. A cable that connects pin 1 to pin 8 (the plugs look the same) puts 3.3 V on BUSY.
- **The cell holder's marks.** The board's `+` is at the bottom (towards USB). If the Keystone holder's own moulded `+` ends up at the top after assembly, trust the board. A cell the wrong way round does no harm (Q1), the light just stays dark.
- **JLCPCB's placement preview.** It shows each part on its pads as the machine will place it: pin-1 dots of the ICs, the USB-C socket's mouth at the edge, and the LEDs' marked ends – on the **resistor side for red and yellow** (their marked pad is the anode), on the **MOSFET side for green** (cathode).

After inserting a cell, plug USB in once: the protection IC may start in its cut-off state and only a charger releases it.

Stock at JLCPCB on 2026-11-27: the 8 MB module 349 (ESP32-C6-MINI-1-H4, C6553337: 1163; -N4, C5736265: 5901 – 4 MB is enough for the firmware), the coil 503, the cell holder 225, the charger 1772, the supervisor 2089. 25 part types are *extended* (a one-off fee each), two more *preferred extended*, 16 basic.

## Magnet mount

Reference spot: the light sits on a **steel newel post of the stair railing** at the end of a ~5 m hallway. The enclosure gets magnet pockets in two places, both optional parameters in the [OpenSCAD model](../enclosure/README.md) (`foot_magnets`, `side_plate`, `side_magnets`):

- **Foot:** the light stands on the post. The magnets keep it from being knocked off; weight keeps it upright.
- **Side wall:** on the railing or handrail, so the light can sit higher, at eye level. Here the magnets carry the whole weight – through friction.

**Check first:** a fridge magnet must stick to the railing. Many railings are stainless steel (austenitic, e.g. 1.4301/304), which is barely magnetic. Then a clamp or a thin steel plate (glued or clamped on) is needed instead.

### Holding force – an estimate

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Mass | ~150 g PETG + ~45 g cell + ~25 g PCB, LEDs, e-ink ≈ **220 g** → 2.2 N |
| Friction on the side wall | printed PETG on painted steel μ ≈ 0.3; with a rubber pad μ ≈ 0.6 |
| Needed pull force, side wall, safety factor 3 | 2.2 N / 0.6 × 3 ≈ **11 N (≈ 1.1 kg)** with a rubber pad; ≈ 2.2 kg without |
| Proposal | **4 × Ø 12 × 3 mm neodymium discs (N42–N52)** in the side wall, 2 in the foot |

Datasheet pull forces (roughly 2.5–3.5 kg for a Ø 12 × 3 mm disc) apply to direct contact with thick, flat steel. A printed skin of 0.6 mm plus a pad, a thin tube wall and a round profile each cost a lot – expect a third to half of the datasheet value. Four discs still leave a margin; the tolerance test print checks it on the real railing.

### Construction

- **Pockets:** blind holes 0.1–0.2 mm larger than the magnet. Either **embedded** – pause the print at the pocket's top layer, drop the magnets in, continue; 0.6 mm (3 layers) of PETG cover them, no glue – or **open** pockets, pressed in and secured with a drop of CA glue or epoxy.
- **Polarity** doesn't matter on steel. Neodymium is fine up to ~80 °C – no issue for PETG or the cell.
- **Pad:** 0.5–1 mm rubber or silicone sheet on the contact face: higher friction (above) and no scratches on the paint. Felt protects too but slides.
- **Shape:** the contact face follows the profile – flat for square posts, a shallow V or radius for round tubes, so that more than one line touches. Post and rail sizes are parameters.
- **Fall protection:** a slot for a cable tie or cord around the post. A falling light on a staircase is a hazard, and it carries a cell.
- **Antenna:** the ESP32-C6 antenna sits at the top edge of the PCB; keep it away from the steel (no magnet wall directly behind it), large steel parts detune and shield it.

## Wi-Fi (UniFi)

Reference network: UniFi Dream Router (Wi-Fi 6) plus a UniFi AC Mesh (Wi-Fi 5) as an access point in the basement.

- **2.4 GHz:** the ESP32-C6 only speaks 2.4 GHz (Wi-Fi 6). The SSID the light uses needs the 2.4 GHz band enabled.
- **TWT** (target wake time, saves battery) exists only with Wi-Fi 6: possible with the Dream Router, not with the AC Mesh. Whether the access point actually grants TWT to the C6 is measured on the breadboard – the light must not depend on it.
- **mDNS** (`doormutex-<name>.local`): works without settings inside one network. If the light lives in its own IoT network/VLAN, enable *Multicast DNS* for the networks of the light, the hub and the phones, and don't let multicast/broadcast filtering on the SSID block it.
- **Battery tuning (optional, measure first):** a higher DTIM period on the 2.4 GHz SSID lets the light sleep longer between beacons; if the light has trouble connecting or roaming, try the SSID without *Fast Roaming* (802.11r).
