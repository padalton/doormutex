# Hardware

The KiCad project for PCB v1 (milestone M1b, after the [breadboard prototype](../docs/breadboard.md)): schematic, layout and the JLCPCB order files, all generated by scripts from one netlist. Not built yet – review before the first order.

## PCB v1

| File | What |
|---|---|
| [`tools/design.py`](tools/design.py) | **the circuit** – every part with its value, LCSC number and the net on each pin. Edit this, not the schematic |
| [`tools/gen_schematic.py`](tools/gen_schematic.py) | writes `doormutex.kicad_sch` from it, runs KiCad's ERC and checks that the exported netlist is exactly the design |
| [`lib/`](lib/) | the JLCPCB parts as a project library: symbols and footprints imported from their LCSC numbers with [easyeda2kicad](https://github.com/uPesy/easyeda2kicad.py), so footprint and placement match what JLCPCB assembles. The 3D models (~40 MB) are not in the repository – the same command with `--full` fetches them into `lib/doormutex.3dshapes/`. [`tools/fix_lib.py`](tools/fix_lib.py) corrects the pin types EasyEDA gets wrong (passive parts marked as inputs) |
| `doormutex.kicad_sch` | generated: one A1 sheet, a block per function, net labels on every pin |
| [`tools/gen_pcb.py`](tools/gen_pcb.py) | the board: outline, 4 layers, every part placed by hand-picked coordinates (`PLACE`), checks that the antenna, the USB-C opening and the INFO button face outwards |
| [`tools/route_pcb.py`](tools/route_pcb.py) | routes it with [Freerouting](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting) (needs Java 25+), ground pours, DRC |
| [`tools/export_jlc.py`](tools/export_jlc.py) | `production/`: Gerber zip, BOM and CPL for JLCPCB |
| `doormutex.kicad_pro`, `doormutex.kicad_dru` | design rules (JLCPCB 4-layer, 0.127 mm), net classes (Power 0.5 mm, USB) |

```bash
python3 hardware/tools/gen_schematic.py      # needs kicad-cli (KiCad 9 or 10)
python3 hardware/tools/gen_pcb.py            # replaces the board – placement lives in PLACE
python3 hardware/tools/route_pcb.py          # Freerouting + DRC (0 errors)
python3 hardware/tools/export_jlc.py         # production/
# a new JLCPCB part:
easyeda2kicad --full --project-relative --output "$PWD/hardware/lib/doormutex" --lcsc_id C2845237
python3 hardware/tools/fix_lib.py
```

```
                ▲                     │      ▲ EN from TPS3840: off below 3.0 V on SYS
                │ VBAT                └─► LEDs (resistor + AO3400A per lamp)
18650 ─ 0 Ω (measure) ─ PTC ─ P-MOSFET (reverse polarity) ─┘
   └─ minus: DW01A + FS8205A ─► P_N ─ 20 mΩ ─► GND   (MAX17055 counts across it)
```

| USB | HRO TYPE-C-31-M-12 (C165948), USBLC6-2SC6 (C7519), 5.1 kΩ on CC | one socket for charging, power and flashing – no more pulling the 3V3 wire to use the console |

| Cell | Keystone 1042 holder (C5249310), PTC 2 A (C207074), DMP2035U reverse polarity (C110499), DW01A + FS8205A (C351410, C908265) | a 0 Ω in the cell's plus lead (and a 2-pin footprint next to it) to measure the current, e.g. with the INA228 logger |

### Layout

41 × 191.5 mm, 4 layers (inner layer 1 a solid ground plane, inner layer 2 for routing), the board is the back wall of the three chambers in the enclosure's PCB build (`build = "pcb"` in [`enclosure/doormutex.scad`](../enclosure/doormutex.scad)).

| Side | What |
|---|---|
| Front (towards the lenses) | the LED pairs in the middle of each reflector cone, the light sensor in a corner of the green chamber (a light channel in the front shell), the e-paper socket in the sign |
| Back | ESP32-C6 at the top with its antenna past the top edge (plastic-only roof), the 18650 holder in the middle, lamp drivers left of it, cell protection and fuel gauge right of it, charger, supervisor and buck-boost at the bottom, USB-C and the side-actuated INFO button on the bottom edge |

- **E-paper:** the Waveshare module hangs upside down (its PH2.0 socket at the top; `menuconfig → E-ink → connector at the top`), the right-angle PH socket J2 sits right above it, opening down. Needed: a **PH2.0 8-pin cable with a socket on both ends, 1:1** (~10 cm). J2 is through-hole and soldered by hand, as is nothing else.
- **Mounting:** four M2.5 self-tapping screws into bosses of the front shell – nylon for the top two, they are 11 mm from the antenna.
- **Assembly:** both sides at JLCPCB (the LEDs and the light sensor are on the front). Check the placement preview: rotations of the KiCad-library parts (USB-C, passives) can differ from JLCPCB's models.

### Before the firmware runs on it

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| e-paper RST → GPIO 14, BUSY → GPIO 15 | the MINI module has no GPIO 10/11 |
| `connector at the top` for the e-ink | the module hangs upside down |
| fuel gauge driver for the MAX17055 (ModelGauge m5 EZ: DesignCap, VEmpty, ModelCfg after every power-on reset) | replaces the MAX17048; same address 0x36 |
| LED reference voltage ≈ 3600 mV, levels per colour from the XINGLIGHT LEDs | LEDs run from SYS (3.0–4.4 V) |
| INFO button on GPIO 5, gauge alert on GPIO 4 | new |

Most ICs are JLCPCB *extended* parts (a one-off loading fee per part type). Low stock when chosen (2026-11-27): the 8 MB module (379) and the inductor (483) – alternatives ESP32-C6-MINI-1-H4 and DFE201610E-R47M.

## Magnet mount

Reference spot: the light sits on a **steel newel post of the stair railing** at the end of a ~5 m hallway. The enclosure gets magnet pockets in two places, both optional parameters in the [OpenSCAD model](../enclosure/README.md) (`foot_magnets`, `side_plate`, `side_magnets`):

- **Foot:** the light stands on the post. The magnets keep it from being knocked off; weight keeps it upright.
- **Side wall:** on the railing or handrail, so the light can sit higher, at eye level. Here the magnets carry the whole weight – through friction.

**Check first:** a fridge magnet must stick to the railing. Many railings are stainless steel (austenitic, e.g. 1.4301/304), which is barely magnetic. Then a clamp or a thin steel plate (glued or clamped on) is needed instead.

### Holding force – an estimate

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Mass | ~150 g PETG + ~45 g cell + ~25 g PCB, LEDs, e-ink ≈ **220 g** → 2.2 N |
| Friction on the side wall | printed PETG on painted steel μ ≈ 0.3; with a rubber pad μ ≈ 0.6 |
| Needed pull force, side wall, safety factor 3 | 2.2 N / 0.6 × 3 ≈ **11 N (≈ 1.1 kg)** with a rubber pad; ≈ 2.2 kg without |
| Proposal | **4 × Ø 12 × 3 mm neodymium discs (N42–N52)** in the side wall, 2 in the foot |

Datasheet pull forces (roughly 2.5–3.5 kg for a Ø 12 × 3 mm disc) apply to direct contact with thick, flat steel. A printed skin of 0.6 mm plus a pad, a thin tube wall and a round profile each cost a lot – expect a third to half of the datasheet value. Four discs still leave a margin; the tolerance test print checks it on the real railing.

### Construction

- **Pockets:** blind holes 0.1–0.2 mm larger than the magnet. Either **embedded** – pause the print at the pocket's top layer, drop the magnets in, continue; 0.6 mm (3 layers) of PETG cover them, no glue – or **open** pockets, pressed in and secured with a drop of CA glue or epoxy.
- **Polarity** doesn't matter on steel. Neodymium is fine up to ~80 °C – no issue for PETG or the cell.
- **Pad:** 0.5–1 mm rubber or silicone sheet on the contact face: higher friction (above) and no scratches on the paint. Felt protects too but slides.
- **Shape:** the contact face follows the profile – flat for square posts, a shallow V or radius for round tubes, so that more than one line touches. Post and rail sizes are parameters.
- **Fall protection:** a slot for a cable tie or cord around the post. A falling light on a staircase is a hazard, and it carries a cell.
- **Antenna:** the ESP32-C6 antenna sits at the top edge of the PCB; keep it away from the steel (no magnet wall directly behind it), large steel parts detune and shield it.

## Wi-Fi (UniFi)

Reference network: UniFi Dream Router (Wi-Fi 6) plus a UniFi AC Mesh (Wi-Fi 5) as an access point in the basement.

- **2.4 GHz:** the ESP32-C6 only speaks 2.4 GHz (Wi-Fi 6). The SSID the light uses needs the 2.4 GHz band enabled.
- **TWT** (target wake time, saves battery) exists only with Wi-Fi 6: possible with the Dream Router, not with the AC Mesh. Whether the access point actually grants TWT to the C6 is measured on the breadboard – the light must not depend on it.
- **mDNS** (`doormutex-<name>.local`): works without settings inside one network. If the light lives in its own IoT network/VLAN, enable *Multicast DNS* for the networks of the light, the hub and the phones, and don't let multicast/broadcast filtering on the SSID block it.
- **Battery tuning (optional, measure first):** a higher DTIM period on the 2.4 GHz SSID lets the light sleep longer between beacons; if the light has trouble connecting or roaming, try the SSID without *Fast Roaming* (802.11r).
